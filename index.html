Nikki Nasseri

I am a Ph.D. student in Information Science and Technology at George Mason University, advised by Dr. Steven Moore, and an NSF Graduate Research Fellow. My research sits at the intersection of learning sciences, psychology, education, and human-AI interaction. I am interested in researching the next tools to guide human learning and communication.

Before GMU, I completed an M.A. in Learning Sciences and Human Development at UC Berkeley and a B.A. in Psychological Science at UC Irvine. My MA thesis investigated how revising AI-guided dialogue prompts in a science unit affected what ninth graders learned, advised by Dr. Marcia Linn. My undergrad honors thesis was on children's verbal engagement with AI conversational agents, advised by Dr. Mark Warschauer. I've also worked as a research intern at the Stanford HAI Institute, building and deploying a full-stack interactive simulation platform to study experiential learning, advised by Dr. Hari Subramonyam.

Outside of research, you can find me mountain biking, skiing, or playing tennis, and I enjoy building personal projects.

MA Thesis · Aug 2025 – May 2026

Revising Adaptive Dialogue Guidance in a WISE Science Unit

  • Studied how revising an NLP-based adaptive guidance dialogue in a WISE science unit affected ninth-grade students' Knowledge Integration gains and idea development
  • Compared two classroom cohorts (original vs. revised prompts) across 191 students using pre/post assessments
  • Found both cohorts made significant learning gains, with revised prompts shifting which specific ideas students developed
  • Complemented quantitative analysis with classroom observations and student interviews
