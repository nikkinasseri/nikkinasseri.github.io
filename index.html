01 About

I'm a first-year PhD student in Information Science and Technology at George Mason University and an NSF Graduate Research Fellow. My research sits at the intersection of learning sciences, psychology, education, and human-AI interaction. I am interested in researching the next tools to guide human learning and communication.

Before GMU, I completed an M.A. in Learning Sciences and Human Development at UC Berkeley and a B.A. in Psychological Science at UC Irvine. My MA thesis investigated how revising AI-guided dialogue prompts in a science unit affected what ninth graders learned. My undergrad honors thesis was on children's verbal engagement with AI conversational agents. I've also worked as a research intern at the Stanford HAI Institute, building and deploying a full-stack interactive simulation platform to study experiential learning.

Outside of research, you can find me mountain biking, skiing, or playing tennis, and I enjoy building personal projects.

03 Research Experience

Stanford HAI Institute

Research Intern

Jun 2025 – Present · PI: Hariharan Subramonyam

Project: Learning by Creating

  • Led a study on how people learn through building simulations versus reading text
  • Designed the study, obtained IRB approval, and managed recruitment & logistics
  • Built a full-stack interactive simulation platform from scratch (React, Node.js, JavaScript), versioned with Git and deployed via Heroku
  • Created pre/post-tests and a study survey to measure learning outcomes and engagement

Berkeley School of Education, UC Berkeley

MA Thesis

Aug 2025 – May 2026

Revising Adaptive Dialogue Guidance in a WISE Science Unit

  • Studied how revising an NLP-based adaptive guidance dialogue in a WISE science unit affected ninth-grade students' Knowledge Integration gains and idea development
  • Compared two classroom cohorts (original vs. revised prompts) across 191 students using pre/post assessments
  • Found both cohorts made significant learning gains, with revised prompts shifting which specific ideas students developed
  • Complemented quantitative analysis with classroom observations and student interviews
